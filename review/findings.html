What six first-time users taught us about the toolbar

Six people tried the labeled prototype in an unmoderated study. Quotes and counts below are from those workshop records, not from a study of the new touch toolbar.

Try the touch toolbar Back to the toolbar review

Top study findings

  1. Repeat drawing was the biggest blocker. Only 3 of 6 completed the five-circles task; the notes say 3 of 6 did not connect “Keep tool” with drawing several in a row. P5 is the one participant quoted on the issue: Keep tool... keep it where?

  2. Labels made tools easier to recognize. 6 of 6 found the rectangle and arrow tools right away and completed the task. The researcher reported that 5 of 6 found every tool on the first try. Two participants explicitly praised labels in the study notes: P1, Oh, they have names now. That's all I needed. P2, I'd turn the words off after a day, but today I need them.

  3. Touch users ran into two different layout problems. 2 of 6 reported a touch-device issue, one each: P4 (tablet), The labels make it wider than my screen. P3 (touch laptop), My finger keeps hitting the one next to the one I want.

  4. Image needs to stay visible, not just move out of More. 5 of 6 completed the photo task; 1 of 6 (P4) couldn't see the button in the scrolling toolbar. P6 is the one participant quoted praising its placement: Image being right there is great. Last time I couldn't find it at all.

The notes also record that 2 of 6 tried the hand tool on a single shape first. There is no participant quote for that observation.

What changed and what to retest

The practice design review picked Playful, and 31 of 40 customers in a separate survey preferred it. The review and survey don't count toward the six-person study. This repo's favorite is Big and touch friendly, so we kept it as the default and applied the feedback to that design. The review called for touch targets of at least 44 pixels, and the accessibility specialist asked that the selected state can't rely on color alone.

“Repeat tool” now says On or Off and has a full-sentence tooltip. Insert image stays on the dock; labeled drawing buttons form a grid on narrow screens. Select still says Objects and Pan still says Canvas. The selected button gets an outline, labels use the theme's primary text color, and there is no new motion. Shortcuts still work; a compact mode and always-visible shortcut hints are out of scope.

Next, repeat the five-circles and photo tasks on a portrait tablet with first-time users. Check whether they can find Repeat tool and Insert image unaided and whether they tap neighboring buttons by mistake.
